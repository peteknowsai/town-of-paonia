A citizen-initiated ordinance

The Ratepayer Protection Ordinance

No hidden formula. Ratepayers approve the plan. And if there is no approved plan, nothing gets charged to your water bill.

Published by Transparent Towns · Paonia, Colorado

Why this is needed

Paonia charges a share of its government payroll and overhead to the Water, Sewer, and Trash funds — the funds your utility bill fills. There is nothing wrong with that in principle; most towns do some version of it. What is wrong is how Paonia does it: through a formula that has never been published, never been presented at a public meeting, and that changes from year to year without explanation.

The results are documented, from the town's own records, in The Story of Paonia's Money. The short version:

  • In 2024 and 2025, most of the cost of the town's administration was moved out of the General Fund — the budget voters watch — and onto the utility funds, through that unpublished formula. The General Fund's "General Government" line fell 76.7% in a single year while the people and the work stayed the same.
  • The utility lines that absorbed those costs were created the same December night in 2023 that the five-year water rate increases passed — twenty-seven minutes apart, with no mention of either in the other's hearing.
  • The rate increases were adopted to fix pipes. The pipe projects keep slipping; the payroll charges arrive on schedule. In 2025, 65 cents of every dollar Trash customers paid went to salaries and benefits, and the fund still lost $42,314.
  • Even the town's own audit bill rides the formula: 82% of the cost of auditing the whole town's books was charged to the utility funds.
  • Hundreds of the customers paying these charges live outside town limits. They pay whatever the formula produces, and they cannot vote for a single member of the Board that produces it.
Every dollar of overhead moved onto the utility funds is a dollar of government that no longer appears in the budget voters control — and a dollar that will come back later as the next rate increase.

This ordinance does not cut anyone's pay. It does not set or cap a single rate. It does one thing: it ends the hidden formula. If the town wants to charge its administration to your water bill, it has to write the formula down, show the basis for it, and get it approved — in public, every year, by a board of the people who pay it.

What it does, in plain English

  • No hidden formula. No salary, benefit, or overhead cost can be charged to the Water, Sewer, or Trash funds except under a written Cost Allocation Plan that names each cost, the basis for the split, and the exact percentages — and explains every change from the year before.
  • Ratepayers approve the plan. A five-member Utility Ratepayer Board — including two seats reserved for customers outside town limits, the people who pay the rates but cannot vote in town elections — reviews and approves the plan each year in a public meeting.
  • The elected Board keeps the last word — but must use it in daylight. If the Ratepayer Board rejects the plan, the Trustees can override it only by a three-quarters vote, with written findings answering each objection, at a noticed public hearing.
  • The default protects ratepayers. No approved plan and no override means no allocation: the overhead stays in the General Fund, where the voters who elect the Board can see it.
  • Violations pay themselves back. Anything charged to a utility fund outside an approved plan must be reimbursed from the General Fund the following year, as a visible line in the budget.

The ordinance

AN INITIATED ORDINANCE OF THE TOWN OF PAONIA, COLORADO, REQUIRING A PUBLISHED COST ALLOCATION PLAN FOR ALL CHARGES TO UTILITY ENTERPRISE FUNDS AND ESTABLISHING A UTILITY RATEPAYER BOARD

Section 1. Findings

The registered electors of the Town of Paonia find that: (a) the Town operates water, wastewater, and solid-waste utilities funded by user fees; (b) charges for shared personnel and general government overhead have been allocated to these utility funds without a published methodology; (c) a substantial share of utility customers reside outside Town limits and have no vote in Town elections; and (d) the Government Finance Officers Association identifies a written, annually reviewed cost allocation plan as the standard practice for allocating shared costs to enterprise funds.

Section 2. Definitions

"Utility Fund" means any enterprise fund of the Town operated for water, wastewater, or solid-waste service, however denominated.

"Allocated Cost" means any portion of a salary, wage, benefit, pension contribution, insurance premium, professional service, or administrative overhead expense that is charged to a Utility Fund and that is not an expense incurred solely and directly for the operation of that utility.

"Cost Allocation Plan" means a written document that identifies each Allocated Cost by budget line; states the percentage of each cost charged to each fund; states the factual basis for each percentage, including time records, transaction counts, or another measurable driver; and reconciles the current year's percentages to the prior year's, explaining every change.

Section 3. No allocation without an approved plan

(a) On and after the first fiscal year beginning after adoption of this ordinance, no Allocated Cost may be charged to any Utility Fund except pursuant to a Cost Allocation Plan approved as provided in Section 5.

(b) Any Allocated Cost charged in violation of this section shall be reimbursed to the affected Utility Fund from the General Fund in the following fiscal year, and the reimbursement shall be stated as a separate line in the budget.

Section 4. Utility Ratepayer Board

(a) There is created a Utility Ratepayer Board of five members: three members who are customers of record of a Town utility residing within Town limits, and two members who are customers of record residing outside Town limits.

(b) Members are appointed by the Board of Trustees from all applicants at a public meeting; if the number of qualified applicants for any seat exceeds one, selection among them shall be by lot. Members serve staggered three-year terms, may not be Town employees, elected officials, or their immediate family, and serve without compensation.

(c) The Board elects its own chair, sets its own meeting schedule, and is subject to the Colorado Open Meetings Law and the Colorado Open Records Act. Town staff shall provide the Board all financial records it requests within ten business days.

Section 5. Annual approval process

(a) No later than September 30 of each year, the Town Administrator shall submit the proposed Cost Allocation Plan for the following fiscal year to the Utility Ratepayer Board and publish it on the Town website.

(b) Within thirty days of submission, the Board shall hold at least one public meeting and vote to approve or reject the plan. Approval requires a majority of the full membership.

(c) If the Board rejects the plan or fails to act within thirty days, the Board of Trustees may adopt the plan only by the affirmative vote of three-quarters of its full membership, at a public hearing noticed at least ten days in advance, upon written findings that respond to each objection stated by the Ratepayer Board.

(d) If no plan is approved under subsection (b) and no override is adopted under subsection (c) before final adoption of the budget, no Allocated Cost may be charged to any Utility Fund in that fiscal year.

Section 6. Transparency

(a) The adopted budget and every budget amendment shall print the Cost Allocation Plan percentages beside each affected line item.

(b) The monthly financial report presented to the Board of Trustees shall state the year-to-date dollar amount of Allocated Costs charged to each Utility Fund.

(c) The Cost Allocation Plan, all time records or other driver data supporting it, and the Ratepayer Board's minutes shall be published on the Town website and retained as permanent records.

Section 7. Severability; conflict

If any provision of this ordinance is held invalid, the remainder continues in effect. To the extent of any conflict with a resolution or administrative practice of the Town, this ordinance controls.

This is normal, not radical

Every comparable Colorado town that charges government costs to its utilities publishes its number. Paonia is the outlier:

TownHow they handle admin costs in utility funds
Colorado SpringsPublished surplus-transfer percentage, adopted in public
LovelandPublished 7% payment in lieu of taxes
LongmontPublished 8.64% franchise equivalent
CrawfordClerk payroll split 50/25/25, in labeled budget lines
DoloresAdministrative salaries in equal published thirds
HotchkissAll General Fund, with visible reimbursement transfers
PaoniaUnpublished formula, changed year to year, never presented

This ordinance asks Paonia to do what its neighbors already do — write the number down and defend it in public — and adds one protection no neighbor needed: a seat at the table for the people who pay the bill.

If the formula is defensible, approving it once a year in public costs the town nothing. If it isn't defensible, that's exactly why this ordinance is needed.

The full evidence behind this ordinance — every number from the town's own audits, budgets, and board packets — is at The Story of Paonia's Money.
